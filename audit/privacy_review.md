# Publication-file content review

The November 5 tree contains public code, non-identifying aggregate results, genomic window inventories, binned LD summaries, synthetic examples, and separate historical/revised figures. Automated checks scan both TSV and CSV tables and reject sample/SNP identifier, genotype, per-variant beta/SE and PIP columns. Raw genotypes, phenotype values, animal IDs, full GRMs, raw LD pairs, detailed GWAS, credible-set membership and per-SNP fine-mapping calls are excluded.

Source provenance uses relative filenames, input roles and checksums. New sources, derived tables, documentation and audit records are checked for private workstation or computing paths. All publication files remain below 10 MB. Full private source inventories, internal plans and obsolete working reports are retained outside this tree. Scientific reference tables and historical estimators remain where required for reproducibility.

The November 11–12 additions include aggregate recovered-effect/PVE-group summaries, final TRUE-run fine-mapping metrics, submission artwork, captions and editable aggregate Table S1. The supervisor's manuscript notes, recovered-effect variant table, detailed lead/PIP records and Manhattan source caches are excluded. Portable calculators accept explicit restricted inputs; their manifests record roles and fingerprints without private source roots. All ten GCTA phenotypes are complete. The November 13 root-summary migration exports all ten configurations at the familiar summary paths and preserves the old 29-file aggregate snapshot under an explicit historical directory. New support tables contain only QC counts, relative source fingerprints and aggregate locus-set overlaps; no detailed loci or SNP identifiers are exported.

This is a content review, not a claim about institutional approval or governing data-access permissions. No new ethics, data-access or funding declaration has been invented. The revision record retains outstanding author-supplied access terms, recovery-algorithm provenance and upstream theoretical-input dependencies.
